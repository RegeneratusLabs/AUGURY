**Tier:** 4 — Environmental Weeds
**Category:** Introduced (Tropical America) — Weed of National Significance (WoNS)
**Indicator Type:** Tropical Wetland Degradation, Floodplain Invasion, Seedbank Persistence

## Indicator Data

- **Tropical Wetland Degradation:** Premier indicator of tropical floodplain wetland degradation in northern Australia — forms dense monospecific thickets that replace all native vegetation.
- **Floodplain Invasion:** Invasion pattern follows floodplains and seasonally inundated wetlands; spread primarily by floodwater seed dispersal.
- **Feral Animal Disturbance:** "Highest densities of outbreaks in wetland margins disturbed by feral animals" — indicator of combined feral animal + weed impact on wetlands.
- **Long-Inundation Wetlands:** Prefers "wetland habitats with a relatively long period of inundation but where trees were absent" — indicator of treeless floodplain vulnerability.
- **Seedbank Persistence:** Seeds remain viable for 22+ years — indicates persistent weed seedbank in wetland soils.
- **Pastoral/Indigenous Impact:** Blocks access to water for stock; prevents harvesting of bush foods by Indigenous people.
- **Distribution:** Currently ~85,000–140,000 ha in NT (Adelaide, Finniss, Reynolds, Mary, Daly, East Alligator, South Alligator, Victoria, Phelp River systems). One outbreak near Proserpine, QLD (2001).

## Weed Status
- Weed of National Significance (WoNS)
- Declared weed in NT and QLD; banned from introduction in WA
- Kakadu National Park: zero-tolerance "search and destroy" program; ~$500,000/year; 4 full-time staff
- Nine insect and two fungal biological control agents released
- Integrated control: herbicides, mechanical, fire, biological, revegetation

## Notes
- Introduced to Darwin Botanic Gardens ~1891; spread accelerated when contaminated sand was used in Top End construction (1950s).
- Seed dispersal: floodwaters, cattle/horses (via droppings), feral animals (mud on bodies), vehicles/machinery.
- Seedbank: seeds remain viable >22 years; sustained follow-up control essential.
- Kakadu National Park's program demonstrates that zero-tolerance management is possible but extremely resource-intensive.
- Has the highest potential distribution of any Australian wetland weed — 4.6 million ha across northern Australia.

**Confidence:** high (comprehensive AU government and academic research documentation)
**Soil Indicators:** Tropical floodplain wetlands, seasonally inundated soils, feral-disturbed wetland margins, persistent weed seedbank

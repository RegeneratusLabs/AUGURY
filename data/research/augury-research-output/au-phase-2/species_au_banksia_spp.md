## Indicator Data

- **Extremely Low Phosphorus:** THE iconic Australian indicator of extremely low-P soils. Banksia leaf P concentrations are an order of magnitude lower than crop plants.
- **Deep Sands:** Many species (e.g., B. attenuata) restricted to deep Bassendean sands — presence indicates ancient, deeply leached sand soils.
- **P-Resorption Proficiency:** Banksia re-mobilises P from senescing leaves to levels (27 µg P/g DM) lower than any other plant species recorded — this extreme adaptation only makes sense on extremely P-poor soils.
- **Soil Age:** Strongest indicator of ancient, unglaciated, deeply weathered soils — the SW Australian landscape where Banksia dominates has been weathering for 250+ million years.
- **Mycorrhizal Status:** Typically non-mycorrhizal — cluster roots replace mycorrhizal function; this strategy only works where P is extremely limiting.
- **Distribution:** Australia-wide (173 species) but centre of diversity in SW Western Australia (kwongan).

## Key Species
- *Banksia attenuata* — Slender Banksia (deep sands)
- *Banksia menziesii* — Firewood Banksia
- *Banksia prionotes* — Acorn Banksia
- *Banksia integrifolia* — Coast Banksia (eastern Australia)

## Notes
- Compound cluster roots (more massive than the simple cluster roots of Hakea).
- Many species are fire-adapted: serotinous cones release seeds after fire ("seeders") or resprout from lignotuber ("resprouters").
- High leaf manganese concentration is a marker of carboxylate-based P mining.

**Confidence:** high (extensive AU academic research at UWA and elsewhere on extreme P-efficiency indicator role)
**Soil Indicators:** Extremely low phosphorus, deep sands, ancient weathered landscapes, nutrient-impoverished soils

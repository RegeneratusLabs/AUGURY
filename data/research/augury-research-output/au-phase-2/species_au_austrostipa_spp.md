## Indicator Data

- **Low Fertility:** Austrostipa species are typically associated with low-fertility native pastures — they do not require high nutrient inputs and persist on poor soils.
- **Recovery from Overgrazing:** Can increase in grazed native pastures as more palatable species are removed; some species are less palatable to stock once mature due to sharp seeds.
- **Soil Types:** Various — some species on coastal sands (A. flavescens, A. stipoides), others on heavy clays, others on loams.
- **Rainfall:** Winter-rainfall dominant zones (southern Australia); good indicator of autumn/winter rainfall response.
- **Distribution:** Widespread across all southern Australian states.

## Key Species
- *Austrostipa nodosa* — Noded Spear Grass
- *Austrostipa flavescens* — Coast Spear-grass
- *Austrostipa drummondii* — Cottony Spear Grass
- *Austrostipa mollis* — Supple Spear-grass
- *Austrostipa stipoides* — Prickly Spear Grass (Coast Spear Grass)

## Notes
- "Self-planting" seeds: the hygroscopic awn twists and drills the seed into soil — an adaptation to arid environments.
- Can be a nuisance in wool production due to seed penetration of sheep skin.

**Confidence:** medium (AU sources confirm distribution and ecology, less formal indicator documentation)

## Indicator Data

- **Waterlogging/High Water Table:** Premier indicator of perennially or seasonally waterlogged soils. QLD RE 7.3.5 description: "water table near or above ground for most of the year."
- **Coastal Swamps:** Forms extensive pure stands in freshwater swamps and around lake margins along the east coast.
- **Poorly Drained Alluvial Plains:** Strong indicator of poorly drained, peaty humic gley soils.
- **Salt Tolerance:** Moderately salt-tolerant; common near coastal beaches and estuarine fringes.
- **Fire Tolerance:** Fire-tolerant; papery bark provides some protection.
- **Distribution:** Coastal eastern Australia — NSW (north from Botany Bay) to Cape York, QLD. Also New Guinea and New Caledonia.

## Notes
- The most familiar "paperbark" in eastern Australia. Distinctive white, papery, multi-layered bark.
- Forms its own vegetation type (Melaleuca swamp woodland/forest).
- Critical role in coastal plain hydrology.
- NOTE: Invasive environmental weed in Florida Everglades but NATIVE in Australia. This file covers its AU-native indicator role.

**Confidence:** high (multiple AU government sources confirm waterlogging/wetland indicator role)
**Soil Indicators:** Waterlogging, high water table, poorly drained alluvial soils, coastal freshwater swamps, peaty humic gley soils

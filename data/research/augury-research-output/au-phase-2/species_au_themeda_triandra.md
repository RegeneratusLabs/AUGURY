## Indicator Data

- **Fire Ecology:** Keystone species for fire management — its presence enables cool, manageable fires under mild conditions. Its decline indicates fire suppression or inappropriate fire regimes.
- **Overgrazing:** Highly sensitive to overgrazing — removal of Themeda is a primary indicator of overgrazed native grasslands.
- **Low Fertility:** Thrives on low-fertility soils; elevated soil nutrients (from fertiliser or weed invasion) disadvantage Themeda.
- **Native Pasture Condition:** Dominance of Themeda indicates high-quality, intact native grassland; its decline is the primary indicator of grassland degradation.
- **Distribution:** Widespread across all Australian states (except perhaps TAS in some classifications); once dominated vast areas of temperate and subtropical grasslands.

## Notes
- Summer-active (C4) perennial tussock grass.
- Cultural significance: important food and fibre plant for Aboriginal peoples across Australia.
- Major restoration target species for endangered temperate grasslands (e.g., Victorian Volcanic Plains, Monaro grasslands).
- "Spray and hay" method developed for restoring Themeda to degraded sites.

**Confidence:** high (extensive AU academic and government research)
**Soil Indicators:** Low fertility, fire-managed landscapes, intact native grassland condition

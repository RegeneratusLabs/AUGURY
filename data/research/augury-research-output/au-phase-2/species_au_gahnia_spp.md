## Indicator Data

- **Wetlands/Damp Sites:** Indicator of damp to wet sites — Gahnia species are reliably associated with moist soils and wetland margins.
- **Soil Type:** Sandy or silty alluvial soils; often in forests with moist understorey.
- **Distribution:** Eastern Australia along the Great Dividing Range; G. sieberiana widespread across eastern states.
- **Ecology:** Many insect larvae feed on Gahnia, making it a biodiversity indicator species.

## Key Species
- *Gahnia sieberiana* — Red-fruit Saw-sedge (widespread eastern Australia)
- *Gahnia aspera* — Rough Saw-sedge (eastern Australia)
- *Gahnia grandis* — Large Saw-sedge (south-eastern Australia, including TAS)

## Notes
- Also known as "cutting grass" — sharp, serrated leaf edges can cut skin.
- Indigenous food plant: leaf bases and seeds edible after processing.
- Tussock-forming habit provides habitat for small fauna.

**Confidence:** medium (AU sources confirm damp/wet site association; limited formal indicator documentation)
**Soil Indicators:** Damp to wet soils, sandy/silty alluvial soils, forest understorey moisture

## Indicator Data

- **Extremely Low Phosphorus:** THE premier indicator of severely phosphorus-impoverished soils. Hakea species occur on some of the most P-impoverished soils in the world (southwest Australia).
- **Ancient Landscapes:** Presence of Hakea indicates ancient, deeply weathered, nutrient-depleted landscapes — southwest Australia has not been glaciated or inundated for 250+ million years.
- **Cluster Roots:** The development of proteoid (cluster) roots is a direct response to low P — cluster roots are suppressed when P is adequate, making their presence on roots a direct indicator of P deficiency.
- **P-Sensitivity:** Many Hakea species are P-sensitive — they suffer toxicity at moderate P levels that would be normal for crop plants. This is itself an indicator: if Hakea are healthy, P is extremely low.
- **Non-Mycorrhizal:** Typically non-mycorrhizal (unlike most plants on low-P soils) — they have evolved cluster roots instead of mycorrhizal associations.
- **Distribution:** Southwest Australia (centre of diversity — ~70% of 150 species endemic to SWAFR); also eastern Australia.

## Key Species
- *Hakea prostrata* — Harsh Hakea (model species for P-efficiency research)
- ~150 species total; mostly SW Australian

## Notes
- Proteaceae is the second-largest plant family in SWAFR (859 species).
- Hakea is nested within Grevillea evolutionarily.
- Cluster roots exude carboxylates (citrate, malate) that "mine" P from soil particles.
- Efficient P remobilisation from senescing leaves and cluster roots.

**Confidence:** high (extensive AU academic research at UWA on P indicator role)
**Soil Indicators:** Extremely low phosphorus, ancient weathered soils, severely nutrient-impoverished landscapes

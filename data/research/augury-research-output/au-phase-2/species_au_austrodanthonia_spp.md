## Indicator Data

- **Low Fertility:** Classic indicator of low-fertility soils — wallaby grasses persist without fertiliser application where introduced pastures would fail.
- **Low Rainfall:** Valuable pasture species in low rainfall zones (300–600 mm); drought-tolerant.
- **Overgrazing Recovery:** Grows well under heavy grazing and recovers after grazing — indicator of native pasture resilience rather than degradation.
- **Soil Type:** Wide range of well-drained soils.
- **Distribution:** Widespread across southern Australia (all states); dominant component of temperate native grasslands.

## Key Species
- *Austrodanthonia caespitosa* (syn. *Rytidosperma caespitosum*) — Common Wallaby Grass
- *Austrodanthonia racemosa* (syn. *Rytidosperma racemosum*)
- *Austrodanthonia setacea* (syn. *Rytidosperma setaceum*)

## Notes
- Now taxonomically placed in genus *Rytidosperma*, but still commonly referred to as Austrodanthonia in pasture literature.
- Domesticated cultivars now available for native pasture sowing (e.g., 'Bunderra', 'Taranna').
- Key species for native grassland restoration and sustainable grazing systems.

**Confidence:** high (NSW DPIRD and lucidcentral AU sources confirm low fertility/recovery indicator role)
**Soil Indicators:** Low fertility, low rainfall, well-drained soils, native pasture resilience

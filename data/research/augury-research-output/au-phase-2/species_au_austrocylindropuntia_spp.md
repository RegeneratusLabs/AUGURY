**Category:** Introduced (South America) — Weed of National Significance (WoNS)
**Indicator Type:** Arid Zone Disturbance, Rangeland Degradation, Human Settlement History

## Indicator Data

- **Arid/Semi-arid Zone:** Key indicator of arid and semi-arid rangeland vulnerability to invasion — cacti are drought-resistant through Crassulacean Acid Metabolism (CAM photosynthesis).
- **Disturbance:** Strong indicator of past human settlement disturbance — "cacti flourish around old homesteads, dumps and mine sites." Most spread in Australia has been by people.
- **Rangeland Degradation:** Dense infestations indicate severe rangeland degradation — compete with native vegetation, limit grazing, harbour pest animals.
- **Water Dispersal:** Spread along watercourses indicates flood-dispersed weed invasion pathways.
- **Soil Type:** No soil preference — found on calcareous loam, shallow granite, red earths, and clay soils. Can establish in >150 mm annual rainfall.
- **Distribution:** All Australian states and territories (except ACT).

## Key Species in Australia
- *Austrocylindropuntia cylindrica* — Cane Cactus
- *Austrocylindropuntia subulata* — Eve's Pin Cactus

## Weed Status
- Weeds of National Significance (WoNS)
- Prohibited import into Australia under Commonwealth Biosecurity Act 2015
- Category 2/3 restricted invasive plants under QLD Biosecurity Act 2014
- Declared weeds in all states
- Biological control: Cactoblastis moth and Dactylopius cochineal insects

## Notes
- Austrocylindropuntia differs from Cylindropuntia in that spines lack papery sheaths.
- Segments can survive indoors for 3 years without soil or water.
- Glochids (tiny barbed bristles) cause serious injury and are difficult to remove from skin.
- The 1920s Cactoblastis biological control success against O. stricta is one of the world's most famous weed biocontrol examples, but it does not control all Opuntioid species.

## Indicator Data

- **Rangeland Degradation:** Classic indicator of rangeland degradation — O. stricta covered 240,000 km² of NSW/QLD at its peak, rendering land unusable for grazing.
- **Historical Introduction:** Presence indicates areas of historical human introduction — originally brought for cochineal dye industry, as garden ornamentals, hedges, and stock fodder.
- **Arid/Semi-arid:** Dominates in low-rainfall areas where competition from other vegetation is reduced.
- **Water Dispersal Pathways:** Spread along watercourses, drainage lines, and flood plains — indicator of flood-dispersed weed invasion.
- **Old Settlement Sites:** "Cacti flourish around old homesteads, dumps and mine sites."
- **Distribution:** All Australian states and territories; most widespread in QLD, NSW, SA, WA rangelands.

## Key Species in Australia
- *Opuntia stricta* (syn. O. inermis) — Common Prickly Pear / Spiny Pest Pear
- *Opuntia aurantiaca* — Tiger Pear
- *Opuntia robusta* — Wheel Cactus
- *Opuntia tomentosa* — Velvety Tree Pear
- *Opuntia monacantha* — Drooping Tree Pear
- *Opuntia elata* — Riverina Pear
- *Opuntia microdasys* — Bunny Ears

## Weed Status
- Weeds of National Significance (WoNS)
- Prohibited imports under Commonwealth Biosecurity Act 2015
- Biological control: Cactoblastis cactorum (moth) highly successful on O. stricta; Dactylopius spp. (cochineal insects) for other species
- The 1926 Cactoblastis introduction is one of history's most successful biological control programs
- Integrated management: chemical, mechanical, biological, and fire

## Notes
- Opuntia has flattened stem segments (pads/cladodes), distinguishing them from cylindrical Austrocylindropuntia and Cylindropuntia.
- Most spread is vegetative — pads detach, root, and form new plants.
- Fruits of some species (O. ficus-indica) are edible, but O. ficus-indica is the only non-declared Opuntia in some jurisdictions.
- The cost of chemical/mechanical control often exceeds the value of infested land.

**Confidence:** high (comprehensive AU government WoNS documentation; one of Australia's most famous weed invasions)
**Soil Indicators:** Rangeland degradation, arid zone disturbance, old settlement/homestead sites, flood-dispersed weed pathways

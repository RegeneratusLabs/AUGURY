## Indicator Data

- **Groundwater Depth:** THE premier Australian indicator species for groundwater depth. Critical thresholds identified at 12.1–22.6 m — beyond this, canopy condition declines abruptly.
- **Groundwater Salinity:** In upper Murray (fresh groundwater <15 mS/cm²), dieback increases with increasing depth. In lower Murray (saline >30 mS/cm²), condition improves with depth as trees avoid saline groundwater.
- **Riparian/Floodplain:** Indicator of floodplain health and hydrological connectivity. Requires periodic flooding for recruitment.
- **Water Availability:** Trees on shallow groundwater (<2–3 m) have greater growth rates and are less reactive to drought.
- **Dieback Indicator:** 70% of Victorian Murray River floodplain forest dying back — condition directly tied to groundwater access.
- **Distribution:** Most widespread eucalypt on mainland Australia; all states except TAS.

## Notes
- Phreatophytic (groundwater-dependent) — facultative user that switches between soil water and groundwater.
- Dimorphic root system allows switching between water sources.
- The quintessential Australian riparian tree; culturally and ecologically significant.
- Massive dieback occurring across Murray-Darling Basin due to river regulation and groundwater decline.

**Confidence:** high (extensive AU academic research specifically on groundwater indicator role)
**Soil Indicators:** Groundwater depth <12 m (healthy), >22 m (dieback threshold), groundwater salinity, floodplain connectivity

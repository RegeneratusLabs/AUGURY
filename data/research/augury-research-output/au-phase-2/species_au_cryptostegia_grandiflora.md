## Indicator Data

- **Riparian Invasion:** Strong indicator of tropical riparian zone vulnerability to weed invasion — rubber vine exclusively establishes first along waterways, then spreads outward.
- **Flood-Dispersed Weeds:** Seed dispersal predominantly by wind and floodwater — presence indicates flood-mediated weed invasion pathways. Seeds remain viable after 1+ month floating in fresh or salt water.
- **Tropical Waterway Degradation:** Indicator of degraded tropical waterways — smothers native riparian vegetation, displaces riverbank fauna, and affects water quality.
- **Pastoral Impact:** 1995 estimated cost to QLD beef industry: $18 million/year — indicator of severe pastoral weed impact in tropical grazing systems.
- **Soil Type:** Grows on all soil types; prefers moist sites with supporting vegetation for climbing.
- **Climatic Range:** 400–1400 mm annual rainfall; tropical and sub-tropical zones.
- **Distribution:** Currently restricted to QLD (700,000 ha in 1991); potential to spread across northern NT, WA Kimberley/Pilbara, and NE NSW.

## Weed Status
- Weed of National Significance (WoNS)
- Category 3 restricted invasive under QLD Biosecurity Act 2014
- Two biological control agents released (1990s): rubber vine rust (*Maravalia cryptostegiae*) and Euclasta moth (*Euclasta whalleyi*)
- Rubber Vine Containment Line established to prioritise control
- Isolated infestations in WA controlled/eradicated

## Notes
- Introduced as ornamental in QLD mining towns (~1860s); also cultivated during WWII as potential rubber source.
- Closely related to *Cryptostegia madagascariensis* (also weedy in Australia).
- Toxic to livestock.
- Fire can be used as a control method but requires hot burns.

**Confidence:** high (comprehensive AU government WoNS documentation)
**Soil Indicators:** Tropical riparian zones, flood-dispersed weed pathways, degraded tropical waterways, pastoral weed impact
